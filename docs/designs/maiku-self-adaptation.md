# Design: maiku self-adaptation, one vocabulary for intelligence and things

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-09-02
Branch: main
Repo: kanishkpaul/maiku
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement

**maiku is the substrate that connects and plays everything with each other.**

Anything that computes or connects should be addable to one system and work with everything
else in it, without a human writing an integration per pairing. Not merely reachable from
one another: composable, in one vocabulary, as peers.

The stated target is the maximum possible version of that, not a viable subset of it.

The scope is deliberately wider than the agent-protocol space: an LLM, a CNN, a closed-form
math model, an MCP server, an HTTP API, a person, a smart bulb, a robot. "Tool" means
anything connectable, not anything callable. maiku adapts itself to the thing rather than
requiring the thing be wrapped for maiku, and the end result is delivered as an app or
webapp, not a library.

The framing moved twice during this session. It was stated first as a universal adapter,
then corrected to a platform, then corrected again to the above. The final statement is the
binding one, and the two earlier framings were both narrower than what is intended.

## Demand Evidence

None. Stated plainly: this is a first prototype, private repo, zero external users, created
2026-08-31.

Two facts that are not demand but are adjacent to it:

- The most recent commit adapts IndicTrans2 through the `cli` adapter specifically because
  it lives in a Python environment maiku cannot import. That is the author solving a real
  problem in a separate research project, unprompted.
- MCP has over 18,000 community-indexed servers and A2A has 150+ supporting organizations.
  The demand for *composition* is demonstrated. The demand for composition *including
  non-chat and physical participants* is not.

The founder was offered the option of claiming himself as the first user and declined,
choosing to build the primitive first. This is recorded as a deliberate choice, not an
oversight.

## Status Quo

Selected during the session: people hand-write a wrapper per system. To call a local model,
a Python module, or an internal service from an agent client today, someone writes an MCP
server for it by hand, with its own schemas and process management, once per system.

On the device side the status quo is different and stronger than the repo's research
currently accounts for. Home Assistant shipped MCP in both directions in 2025: a built-in
server exposing devices, states, areas and intents as MCP tools, and a client so LLM
conversation agents can drive the house. It has a decade of integrations behind it.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user: not yet named.** The founder explicitly chose "nobody specific yet, I'm
building the primitive first."

**Wedge: explicitly refused.** Every narrowing offered during the session (MCP policy plane,
CLI-adapter-only, governance-first) was rejected on the grounds that universality is the
product and narrowing it produces an appliance. Recorded as a positioning decision, not an
unanswered question.

The counter-argument, for the record: primitive-first has succeeded (Git, Docker) and failed
(UDDI, any-agent), and the distinguishing variable in every case was whether the author was
himself the desperate user. That variable is currently unset here.

## Constraints

- Solo builder, at a build velocity that invalidates conventional effort estimates.
  Verified from the commit log: the entire current codebase, 27 commits covering nine
  adapters, three projections, two gateways, the runtime, the conformance suite and the
  docs, was built between 11:13 and 12:49 on 2026-08-31. **96 minutes for 10,581 lines of
  `src` plus `tests`.** Any estimate in this document phrased in human-team weeks should be
  read as a statement about scope, not about schedule.
- Hardware access via friends. Physical devices are not a procurement problem, and the
  domain knowledge for commissioning and pairing is reachable. This removes what would
  otherwise be the single hardest-to-compress input to the physical half.
- Closed source. No open-sourcing of the runtime. The open/closed split for the descriptor
  and adapters is left as an open question, not settled.
- Existing asset: 7,132 lines of `src/`, 3,449 lines of tests, 9 adapter schemes, 2 gateways,
  3 projections, one conformance contract every adapter passes.
- Zero lines of UI. `git ls-files` matches no HTML, JS, TS, CSS, Svelte or Vue file.
  `gateways/http_server.py` is 77 lines.
- `AffordanceKind.PROPERTY`, `AffordanceKind.EVENT` and `ParticipantKind.ROBOT` are defined
  and have **zero usages** across `src/`, `tests/` and `examples/`. The half of the
  vocabulary that covers physical things has never been exercised.

## Premises

Agreed during the session. 1, 2, 3 and 5 accepted; 4 accepted as a live decision rather than
a settled premise.

1. maiku is a governed composition substrate, not a universal adapter. The adapters are
   inventory acquisition; the runtime is the product.
2. The defensible asset is chained, attenuating, verifiable authority over heterogeneous
   participants, plus one vocabulary in which a model and a physical thing are peers.
3. Supply for the chat-shaped half is free by ingesting MCP, A2A, OpenAPI and CLI. Supply
   for the non-chat and physical half, which is the actual differentiation, is not free.
4. **(open)** Closed is right for the runtime. Whether the descriptor and adapters should be
   open is undecided. The conformance contract is what would make open contribution safe
   rather than fragmenting, which is an unusually strong argument for a split.
5. The clock is MCP's spec roadmap. The 2026-07-28 spec rewrote MCP's foundation around
   OAuth 2.1, RFC 8693 token exchange and resource indicators, and current best-practice
   language is "scope must narrow at each hop." If capability attenuation lands natively in
   MCP, premise 2's governance half expires. The physical-participant half does not.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: The First Bulb (not chosen)
Adapt one real device through the existing binding machinery, give it genuine `property` and
`event` affordances, and put it in one session with the CNN and Gemma already running.
Rejected as a demo of a mechanism that does not exist yet.

### Approach B: The Self-Adaptation Engine (CHOSEN)
Generalize descriptor derivation into a pluggable prober layer. Point maiku at an address and
it attempts WoT Thing Description, OpenAPI, an MCP handshake, Matter and Zigbee cluster
descriptors, the Home Assistant entity registry, and mDNS/SSDP discovery, then emits a
descriptor. "maiku adapts to it" becomes a mechanism rather than a claim.

### Approach C: The App Shell (not chosen)
Build the webapp the product description requires, on the runtime and nine adapters that
already exist. Rejected as building the window before knowing what is outside it.

## Recommended Approach

**Chosen: B. This was chosen against the office-hours recommendation of A-then-C, deliberately.**

The recommendation was A-then-C on the grounds that four days of work would test the
load-bearing assumption under both other paths. The founder's counter-position, which the
doc records as the operative one: self-adaptation is the product claim, and both A and C are
demonstrations of a mechanism that does not exist. Building either first means building a
demo and a UI against a derivation story that has not been designed.

**Scope line, agreed during the session.** "Self-adaptation" is bounded to derivation from a
*declared* interface. maiku already does exactly this nine times over: a Python signature, an
OpenAPI operation, an MCP tool annotation. Extending it to WoT Thing Descriptions, Matter and
Zigbee clusters, and the Home Assistant entity registry is the same move again.

Explicitly out of scope: inferring affordances from an undocumented protocol, or inferring
what a device means. That is the twenty-year automatic-service-composition graveyard the
project's own LANDSCAPE.md already refuses to enter, and the refusal stands.

**Risks, after correction.** The office-hours recommendation of A-then-C rested on an
assumption the repo disproves: that choosing the wrong order is expensive, because whatever
is built first hardens and everything else gets built against it. At 96 minutes for the
current codebase, rewriting is cheap, the cost of choosing wrong collapses, and the argument
for testing cheaply before committing largely goes with it. The "XL, roughly three months"
label originally attached to B was calibrated to a pace this project does not run at, and is
withdrawn.

What survives the correction:

- **The physical half, reduced but not eliminated.** This was originally recorded as the one
  risk build speed cannot compress: hardware acquisition, Matter commissioning, Zigbee
  pairing, vendor auth. Access to hardware through friends removes the acquisition lead time
  and most of the domain-knowledge cost. What remains is per-vendor protocol quirk and auth
  flow, which is real but is now a matter of hours per ecosystem rather than a structural
  blocker. Treat borrowed hardware as a scheduling dependency on someone else's calendar,
  which is the only wall-clock item left.
- **Frame stability.** The framing moved twice inside one session. B is the option least
  tolerant of a third move, though cheap rewriting softens this too.
- **Prober correctness, not prober quantity.** Writing twelve probers fast is easy at this
  velocity. Twelve probers that agree on identity, ordering and conflict resolution is a
  design problem that speed does not solve. See open question 2.

**What de-risks it**, and should be treated as a requirement rather than a nicety: the
conformance contract at `tests/conformance/contract.py` already exists and is the mechanism
that stops many probers becoming many dialects. Every prober must be written to it from the
first one, not retrofitted.

## Open Questions

1. **The open/closed split (premise 4).** Descriptor and adapters open, runtime and registry
   closed, is the Docker shape and the repo's own LANDSCAPE.md line already argues for it.
   Undecided. It is a one-way door in one direction only.
2. **Prober ordering and conflict.** When an address answers to both an MCP handshake and
   OpenAPI, which descriptor wins, and does the participant get one identity or two? The
   content-hash identity model makes this a correctness question, not a preference.
3. **Where the physical half is proven.** B produces a derivation engine. Nothing in B alone
   produces the trace where a CNN, a person and a bulb are peers, which is the differentiated
   claim. This needs a scheduled point, or it will not happen.
4. **App architecture.** Zero lines of UI exist and the product is described as an app. Not
   addressed by B at all.
5. **Home Assistant's position.** Competitor, substrate, or first ingest target. It is absent
   from LANDSCAPE.md and is the strongest incumbent on the physical side.

## Success Criteria

- `maiku add <address>` produces a valid, self-verifying descriptor for a system in an
  ecosystem that has no hand-written adapter, with no human editing the result.
- The probed descriptor passes the existing conformance contract unmodified.
- At least one probed participant exercises `AffordanceKind.PROPERTY` or
  `AffordanceKind.EVENT`, moving those from zero usages to non-zero.
- At least three distinct ecosystems are covered by probers written to one contract, with no
  ecosystem-specific escape hatch in the runtime.
- Failure is legible: when probing fails, maiku reports what it tried and what it got, rather
  than emitting a guessed descriptor. Guessing types nobody stated is the failure mode the
  project already named in SPEC.md.

## Distribution Plan

Deferred deliberately. The repo is private, has no LICENSE file, no CI, no tags and no
package. The stated end state is an app or webapp, which implies a hosted product rather
than a package, and that decision is downstream of open question 1.

One item that is live now regardless: `pyproject.toml:6` declares
`license = { text = "Apache-2.0" }` and no LICENSE file exists. If a wheel is ever built or
the repo is ever made public, that declaration travels with the package metadata and gives
the work away. One-line fix, not urgent while the repo stays private.

## Dependencies

- No blockers. Everything B needs exists: `adapters/introspect.py`, `adapters/openapi.py`,
  `adapters/registry.py`, `adapters/uri.py`, `tests/conformance/contract.py`.
- Hardware for any Matter, Zigbee or physical prober, available through friends. Nothing on
  the physical side can be tested without at least one real device in hand, and this is the
  only dependency that sits on someone else's schedule.
- Open question 1 gates nothing in the next month.

## The Assignment

Before writing a single line of prober code, hand-write the descriptors you want maiku to
derive.

Pick three real endpoints from three different ecosystems that you do not control. One
public WoT Thing Description. One Home Assistant instance, your own or a demo one. One
physical device you own or can buy this week. For each, write by hand the exact
`ParticipantDescriptor` you would want `maiku add` to produce, including affordance kinds,
bindings and permissions.

Message the hardware friend this week and get one real device into your hands, ideally
something that speaks Matter or exposes a WoT Thing Description. It is now the only input on
a calendar other than your own, which makes it the only thing that can silently slip while
everything else moves at your pace.

Two hours, no code. It converts self-adaptation from a claim into a specification, and it is
the fastest available test of whether one vocabulary genuinely spans a Thing Description and
a CNN. If any of the three will not fit the descriptor without an extension, you have found
the real work before spending three months on the wrong derivation target.
